## Big swings

**1. EQS: equity screener over stored XBRL** (L, value 9)
The step-change: the terminal goes from a per-ticker viewer to a universe tool. Screen all 12 holdings plus the 162-name SEG watchlist on any stored fundamental, plus P/E and P/S computed from PriceBar close times dei shares outstanding. Requires the first real persistence of secFundamentals: today it is a 300-entry in-memory Map (secFundamentals.js:28-29), so this build creates the FundamentalSnapshot table fed by the 22:15 pre-warm cron (verified caveat: that cron currently warms people/descriptions only and must be extended). Honest posture vs Bloomberg EQS: roughly 175 names not 40,000, as-filed data, no consensus fields, and every number traces to a specific accession. Verified live: companyfacts serving from data.sec.gov, diluted EPS already extracted, no screener panel in the registry. Side effect that compounds: persisted fundamentals make RV and future valuation work cheaper.

**2. The 10-K redline (Lazy Prices)** (L, value 9)
The step-change: the club becomes the reader nobody else is. Diff this year's Item 1A and Item 7 against last year's, score change magnitude deterministically (cosine/Jaccard), and let the LLM summarize only the changed passages, each pinned to before/after excerpts string-matched into both filings so it cannot invent. Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen (Journal of Finance 2020) show the changes themselves predict returns, up to 188 bps/month changers vs nonchangers, concentrated in MD&A, because nobody reads the diff. Verification says L honestly, not the submitted M: prior-year retrieval is new work (getLatestFilingByForm at secFilings.js:150 returns only the newest filing), the Item 1A/Item 7 extractors do not exist yet, and the panel costs both clients. Build those extractors once and two follow-ons unlock cheap: SENT stage 1 becomes a genuine M, and thesis-drift v2 (currently blocked because the redline it cites does not exist in the repo) becomes buildable.

**3. 13F institutional ownership beyond SEG** (L, value 8)
The step-change: "who else owns our names and what did the smart money just do" answered from primary filings, generalizing the hand-scraped SEG provenance into a real per-manager 13F reader with quarter-over-quarter diffs. The historical blocker, CUSIP-to-ticker mapping, is solved for free by the SEC's own fails-to-deliver files. The whole chain was probed live: data.sec.gov submissions lists Berkshire's 13F-HR accession, the Archives infotable XML parses with per-position CUSIPs, and the FTD zip (200, 1.3MB) carries settlement date, CUSIP, and symbol columns. Build the per-CIK reader, which absorbs the killed HDS idea's goals without the multi-gigabyte bulk path, and label everything with the 45-day staleness inherent to 13Fs.
